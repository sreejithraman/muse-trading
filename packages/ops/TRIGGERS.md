# Check-in tripwires

One routine (`checkin.py`), three ways to invoke it. The tripwires below are
the complete list of what can trigger a check-in — "whenever you want" runs
on these, not on vibes.

## 1. Scheduled (the backbone)

| Check | When (ET) | Mode | Behavior |
|---|---|---|---|
| Morning session | 9:40 AM weekdays | full | Trades. The main decision session. |
| Midday circuit check | 12:30 PM weekdays | light | Silent unless verdict is alert/act. |
| Close review | 3:50 PM weekdays | — | Marks the book; no new entries. |

## 2. Alert-driven (the hook)

`circuit-watch` polls public quotes every 15 min, weekdays 9:30 AM–4:00 PM ET,
against `~/hooks/state/watchlist.json` (regenerated by the morning session via
`checkin.py --emit-watchlist`). Wakes an agent on:

- **breach**: price at or below a thesis red line → verdict `act`
- **approach**: price within 2% above a red line → verdict `alert` (once per
  symbol per day — deduped in hook state)
- **drop**: intraday move ≤ −5% vs previous close → verdict `alert`
  (once per symbol per day)

The woken agent runs `checkin.py --mode light`, triages news on flagged names
(breach on real news ≠ breach on noise), and then:

- `act` → verify, then act under circuit-breaker + standing trading authority;
  report what was done in the what/why/exposure/next shape. Standing authority
  means decide-and-act, then report — never message the user to ask whether to
  act. The only pre-action messages: the needed action is outside standing
  authority, or order placement is technically blocked.
- `alert` → ambiguous: log and stand down silently unless it needs reporting
  after the fact. Do not ask the user what to do.
- `quiet` → stay silent.

## 3. Discretionary one-offs

Any session (or the main agent in chat) may schedule a one-off check-in with
a stated reason — e.g. "recheck ARWR at 2 PM ahead of the webinar" or
"premarket looks wild, run the light check at 8:30." Background research
flags (proactivity pipeline) may also trigger one. One-offs are single-shot
crons, never new recurring schedules, and they die after firing.

## Notes

- Resting native orders (stops, take-profits) are the faster layer: they act
  in seconds while this system acts in minutes. Both exist on purpose.
- Options positions are not covered by price tripwires in v1 (their red lines
  are premium-based); the GRAB-call rule (−50% premium or thesis break) is
  checked by the sessions.
- The falsification clause for adding a midday check is retired — the check
  now exists.

## Data failures are not "quiet"

`checkin.py` emits verdict `unknown` (with `data_quality: degraded/none`)
when quotes can't be fetched for a holding. Unknown is never a clean bill
of health — a blind read reported as "all clear" would be this system
becoming the "just words" it was built to replace.

- Never treat `unknown` as `quiet`. Never report "all clear" on a blind read.
- The agent must retry once via the alternate quote source (the hook uses
  Yahoo; `checkin.py` uses Robinhood MCP). If the book still can't be
  checked, the outage itself becomes the finding: log it, and include it in
  the next user-facing report as a machinery failure. That is report-worthy,
  not silent.
- Resting native orders still protect the book during a data outage — they
  don't depend on this system.
- The `circuit-watch` hook reports `quotes unavailable` (with a consecutive-
  failure streak) instead of `all clear` when Yahoo returns no quotes during
  market hours, so an outage is visible in the hook log rather than
  masquerading as a clean check.
